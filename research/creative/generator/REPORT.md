## Five narrow mini-specs

### I23 — Correct the proof before adding claims

**What:** a Pitch-owner request covering whole-design tie, lead-time versus labor, latency freshness, oracle roof wording, transfer scope, and hard-ceiling phrasing. **How:** reconcile each sentence with `docs/PITCH.md` and current aggregate reports; change no numbers based on a preferred story. **Measure without overfitting:** source-to-claim audit with a zero-unqualified-claim checklist, not a new model comparison. **Demo:** “We win clearly on PV; whole design ties habit. Here is what the product adds.” **Cut line:** stop once these statements are accurate; no deck redesign, new benchmark or new UI.

### I02 — One useful question in the existing workflow

**What:** a reproducible demonstration of the existing Ask next. **How:** choose a synthetic supported home before testing, show unknown/default facts, answer a question with synthetic entered evidence, and observe design/price sensitivity. The model's question score is a heuristic based on hypothetical answer weights and price swing, not calibrated expected accuracy. **Measure:** replay consistency and correct provenance; separately cite the published masking result, without pretending the demo validates question utility. Do not search 2026 homes for a flattering example. **Demo:** planner changes one fact and gets a grounded next draft. **Cut line:** 30 minutes rehearsal; if the response fails, use the clearly labelled offline fixture and disclose it.

### I03 — Identical number, different evidence

**What:** paired audit of 4500 kWh represented as genuine entered/extracted evidence versus installer/national default. **How:** same synthetic home and numeric value; only provenance changes; inspect Ask next, flags and returned provenance. CG shows `_entered` skips entered/extracted questions, while `design_policy` adds `DEMAND_DEFAULT_LIKE` for every 4500 value. That is a concrete possible inconsistency, not proof that sizing changes. **Measure:** all paired synthetic requests retained with expected semantics frozen first; no private labels required. **Demo:** a real 4500 bill is respected while an unconfirmed default is queried. **Cut line:** deliver aggregate evidence and an Engine request; owner edits engine, no threshold inference from demand equality.

### I13 — Incremental independent stress coverage

**What:** extend evidence beyond the already reported fuzz result with explicit schema rejection, finite extremes, locks, existing assets, provenance and review cases. **How:** intake-only generation and independent units/roof/lock assertions, frozen expected accept/reject/review actions, all expected accepted cases in the denominator. Existing `rules.audit` alone cannot independently certify its own implementation. **Measure:** eligible/pass/fail/blocked/not-applicable per rule; no synthetic expert-match accuracy. No annual conservation claim from typical-day arrays. **Demo:** show one exact lock preserved and one contradictory request flagged or rejected; summarize failures honestly. **Cut line:** unsupported hourly-trajectory/BOM/semantic checks remain BLOCKED; never convert missing instrumentation into a pass.

### I04 — The physics second reader can disagree

**What:** explain why the installer-like battery and a simulated economic choice may differ. **How:** use the existing `physics.economicKwh`, metrics and battery sweep for a frozen synthetic design; attach price/weather/load/efficiency/version assumptions. CG `attach_metrics` already exposes without-battery/economic/max-independence variants and may fall back to incomplete metrics after simulation errors. **Measure:** complete metrics and deterministic replay; measured-profile sensitivity is independent evidence, not real savings validation. **Demo:** “This is what the installer usually offers; under these assumptions the smaller battery has higher simulated NPV. The planner chooses.” **Cut line:** no promise of real optimum, no new component sizing source, no bespoke savings claim if assumptions cannot be shown.

## Method and morphological box

Distribution-first prompting is motivated by Zhang et al.'s Verbalized Sampling [S1]. The present probabilities answer the user's **typical-team likelihood** instruction, a pragmatic adaptation; they are not the model probability distribution in the paper and do not reproduce its experiment. The lens pools were kept separate until consolidation. Liang et al. motivate an adversarial critique rather than repeated self-reflection [S2]. The separate critic challenged every shortlisted candidate; the generator accepted the restrictions and sent explicit amendments. Elo-style tournament ranking is inspired by co-scientist, not an empirical estimate of business value [S3].

Si et al. found novelty and feasibility can diverge in an NLP research-idea study, which motivates our repository feasibility pass; their result does not establish energy-domain productivity [S4]. Doshi and Hauser studied short-story outputs and observed greater similarity with AI assistance; this is a diversity warning, not a universal theorem about teamwork [S5]. We retain wildcards even if they rank poorly.

## Kill list

1. New 3D roof/layout/UI before freeze: no new UI is authorized, and this is already a mature competitor feature [S6,S7,S8]. A short workflow proof has lower integration risk.
2. LLM choosing sizes or public inference on private data: forbidden. `research/experiments.md` still lists a Haiku sizing candidate; ask its owner to remove/mark prohibited, do not execute it.
3. Repeated 2026 test tuning, threshold searches, or favorable-example selection: breaks the evaluation protocol. A final exam already inspected cannot honestly be called pristine in a new selection exercise.
4. “Data ceiling is 25%”: not proven. Exact empirical collisions can constrain this finite sample; near-identical demand bands omit distinctions.
5. Any-option match as headline: extra options can mechanically improve it; diagnostic only unless the signed target is genuinely unresolved.
6. MaStR histogram as installation quality or expert-match accuracy: register distributions are contextual plausibility evidence; residential-like filters and PV/storage linkage must be verified.
7. Savings band presented as probabilistic certainty: scenario ranges are conditional on analyst assumptions, not calibrated probabilities.
8. Full Pareto optimizer, novel dynamic-tariff controller, HP engineering certification, energy passport: research/pitch wildcards after freeze, no new implementation this shift.
9. Strong universal transfer claim from two installers: report exactly two leave-one-installer transfers.
10. Labor savings inferred from request-to-offer elapsed days: measure actual staff effort prospectively.

## Likely jury questions and honest answers

| Question | Answer to use |
|---|---|
| Why does the median tie the whole bundle? | Installer package habits and incomplete inputs make a strong baseline. We improve PV agreement; whole-design superiority is not established. |
| Is the cap really 25%? | No. Missing roof and budget explain ambiguity, but the exact ceiling is unproven. Our collision audit is an empirical diagnostic. |
| Would another metric make you look better? | We keep whole-design tolerance fixed as primary and report predefined metric sensitivities on identical predictions, including those unfavorable to us. |
| Are defaults detected from 4500 alone? | That value is only default-like. A real bill can also say 4500. Provenance should govern questioning, and we are auditing inconsistent flags. |
| Does asking the roof really improve by 12 points? | That published result uses the expert module count as an oracle cap, so it is an upper bound, not measured real-roof acquisition. |
| Does the simulator prove the smaller battery is best? | It is best within that scenario and candidate set under stated costs/profiles, not a certified real-world optimum. |
| Is it useful if 4 out of 5 bundles differ? | It drafts, exposes missing inputs, preserves planner choices, compares physics, and explains approved alternatives. Actual time and commercial benefit need prospective measurement. |
| Can it design a heat pump safely? | It provides a physics-led draft with sparse training evidence; an installation needs proper engineering and site facts. We do not claim a certified heat-loss calculation. |
| Does it work for a third installer? | We have two installers and two transfer directions. Broader generalization remains unverified. |
| Why not use an LLM end to end? | Private data remains local and the sizing path is deterministic learned policy plus rules; language models only parse or phrase permitted content. |
| Is your register/profile benchmark accuracy? | No. It checks contextual plausibility and scenario robustness; only labelled installer designs can measure expert agreement. |
| How does this beat existing software? | We demonstrate this prototype's learned installer policy and missing-information workflow. We have no independent head-to-head product test. |
